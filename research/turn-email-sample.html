Day 1 · 07:00 · morning

You ask the question into the cold and nothing comes back.

Not a refusal. There is no one here to refuse you. The trail is a single narrow line of damp gravel along the crest of a ridge, eight degrees out, a light wind moving past the shells of your ears, and it is empty in both directions as far as your eyes carry. You are standing on it. You are the only one standing on it.

So the question goes out and comes back to you with its hands empty.

Who am I.

You wait for yourself to know. The knowing does not arrive as a memory. Nothing comes forward. No road before this one. No face. No hour before the hour you are standing in. What comes instead is a list, and it is short, and every item on it is something you can feel from inside your own body: the cold in your fingers, a small hollow under your sternum that means hunger, a heaviness in the shoulders that is not from walking, because there is no walking to remember. You are facing down-trail. You cannot say why that is the direction you are pointed. You cannot say how long you have been pointed this way.

Then your left hand is up at your cheek.

You did not decide to move it. A fingertip finds something small and round and wet, sitting just under the angle of your jaw. Not running, not dripping. It holds itself together in one bead. You touch it, and your finger comes away wet, and that is the whole of the report: wet, and recent, and carrying no heat of its own. You were half expecting heat. There is none. The skin around it is only skin. It does not sting. It does not itch. It is a mark, and your face has not been told anything useful about who left it there or when.

Your cheek has not told you your name.

Your hand comes down, and there is something at your feet.

It is standing on the gravel, a little ahead of your boot. Clear-sided, a little under half full, cap off, upright, and there is nothing else on this ridge. Nothing else at all. It is close enough that you could put your hand on it without moving from where you are, and you do not, because there is no reason on offer for touching it yet. No scuff in the gravel. Nothing to say it was put down rather than left. You cannot remember doing either.

You are still standing there when you finally look out.

In front of you the trail drops away, one steep descent straight down toward the fog, and it is gone past the point where the gravel changes colour. Behind your other shoulder the switchbacks climb the ridge the other way. Below, the fog is not weather. It fills the whole valley and goes on past the horizon in every direction, flat from here to there with no seam in it, no ripple, no depth. The light lies on that white the way light lies on water. Nothing crosses it. No shadow travels through. If anything is standing down there, you have no way of telling, and no way of not telling, and the ridge is the only piece of ground you can confirm.

The bottle is still standing where it was. Whether the mark on your cheek has dried or has not, you cannot check by looking, so you do not.

You still do not know your name. You still do not know how you came to be standing here next to a bottle somebody left.

Nothing on the ridge has told you yet.
